01 / The actual difference

The chain didn't get simpler. The interface finally caught up.

Swapping, bridging, and staking were never hard because blockchains are complicated. They're hard because doing them safely means checking rates across tabs, guessing parameters, and hoping you didn't miss a step. Here's the same staking deposit, done two ways.

Without an agent

manual
  1. Open the protocol's own site and connect your wallet
  2. Find the right pool for your asset, out of several listed
  3. Open another tab to check whether the rate is actually competitive
  4. Approve the token | a separate transaction, separate gas
  5. Set the parameters yourself and hope they're right
  6. Sign, and wait without knowing if anything went wrong
  7. Open an explorer afterward to confirm it actually worked
≈ 7 steps · 2–3 tabs · full attention required · ≈ 3 minutes

With Lyra

agent
  1. Say what you want, in one sentence
  2. Lyra researches the real rate, fees, and risk, and prepares a ticket
  3. You check it, then hold one button to confirm
3 steps · 1 screen · fully automated · ≈ 3 secondes

04 / How it thinks

It doesn't answer from memory. It goes and checks.

Every ticket Lyra shows you comes from the same three-step loop | no cached rates, no assumed contract addresses, nothing invented to fill a gap.

01

Understand

Lyra reads what you're asking, in plain language, and can piece it together across several messages if you didn't give every detail at once.

02

Research

It searches the web and checks the contract directly on-chain : live, every time, never from memory. If it can't find a reliable answer, it says so.

03

Propose

You get a ticket you can read line by line : rate, fees, risk. Nothing is signed or sent until you hold the button yourself.

06 / Security model

An agent that can propose anything and move nothing.

Automation and custody are two different problems. Lyra solves the first, your device still solves the second.

Your key stays yours

Encrypted locally on your device. Lyra never has access to it, unlocked or not.

It never signs itself

Every action, including withdrawing from staking, waits for you to hold the button.

Locks itself automatically

10 minutes of inactivity and Lyra locks, no setting to remember to turn on.

Verified contracts only

If an address isn't confirmed, Lyra says so instead of guessing and signing anyway.

Optional 2-of-2 key split

Turn it on when you create your wallet: your key is split in two halves, one on your device and one held by Lyra's backend. Neither half can sign alone. Early access.

Spending limits

A cap you set on x402 payments and checkouts, enforced before anything is signed, plus an optional biometric re-check above an amount you choose.

Multisig with Safe

Set up a shared wallet with up to five owners and an approval threshold, like 2 of 3. Nothing moves until enough owners approve.

An audit trail

A local log of what was sent, paid, and delegated, including what got blocked, so you can always see what happened.

Privacy Policy

Last updated: September 2026

1. What we don't collect

Lyra is non-custodial: your private key and wallet data are encrypted and stored locally on your device. We never have access to your seed phrase or funds, and never to a whole private key. If you turn on the optional 2-of-2 protection, our backend holds one encrypted half of a split key: on its own, that half cannot sign anything or reveal your key.

2. What we may collect

  • Information you provide directly, such as through our contact form (name, email, message).
  • Basic technical data (app version, general error logs) used only to diagnose issues.
  • The text of what you type to the assistant, and wallet context needed to act on it, your public wallet address and displayed balance. Never your private key or recovery phrase, which stay local to your device and are never transmitted anywhere.
  • Live queries made on your behalf to check rates, routes, or contract data, processed at the moment of your request and not used to build a profile of you.
  • If you turn on the optional 2-of-2 protection: your public wallet address, the encrypted key half, and the daily spending limit you set, so the backend can enforce it.

3. How we use it

Any data we collect is used solely to operate, maintain, and improve Lyra, and to respond to inquiries sent through our contact form. We do not sell your personal data. Our backend does not maintain a database of your requests: each one is processed to generate a response and is not retained afterward beyond standard, short-lived server logs used for debugging.

4. AI providers

To understand your request and prepare a ticket, Lyra sends what you typed, and the minimal wallet context described above, to an AI provider. Depending on availability, this may be OpenRouter (routing to a Qwen model), Mistral AI, Google (Gemini), or Anthropic (Claude); only one provider handles a given request, chosen automatically as a fallback chain, never all at once. When you pay for a service over x402, the response it returns may also be sent to that provider so it can be summarized in plain language for you. Each provider processes that request under its own privacy policy and may briefly retain it per their own data-handling terms; Lyra does not control this.
