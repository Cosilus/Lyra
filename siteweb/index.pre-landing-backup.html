The actual difference

The chain didn't get simpler. The interface finally caught up.

Swapping, bridging, and staking were never hard because blockchains are complicated. They're hard because doing them safely means checking rates across tabs, guessing parameters, and hoping you didn't miss a step. Here's the same staking deposit, done two ways.

Without an agent

manual
  1. Open the protocol's own site and connect your wallet
  2. Find the right pool for your asset, out of several listed
  3. Open another tab to check whether the rate is actually competitive
  4. Approve the token | a separate transaction, separate gas
  5. Set the parameters yourself and hope they're right
  6. Sign, and wait without knowing if anything went wrong
  7. Open an explorer afterward to confirm it actually worked
≈ 7 steps · 2–3 tabs · full attention required · ≈ 3 minutes

With Lyra

agent
  1. Say what you want, in one sentence
  2. Lyra researches the real rate, fees, and risk, and prepares a ticket
  3. You check it, then hold one button to confirm
3 steps · 1 screen · fully automated · ≈ 3 secondes
What it does

Four real protocols, four chains. One agent deciding what's actually safe to sign.

Lyra doesn't simulate DeFi. It's wired directly into real, verified contracts across Plasma, Ethereum, Base, and Polygon, and checks each one live instead of trusting a cached number.

How it thinks

It doesn't answer from memory. It goes and checks.

Every ticket Lyra shows you comes from the same three-step loop | no cached rates, no assumed contract addresses, nothing invented to fill a gap.

01

Understand

Lyra reads what you're asking, in plain language, and can piece it together across several messages if you didn't give every detail at once.

02

Research

It searches the web and checks the contract directly on-chain : live, every time, never from memory. If it can't find a reliable answer, it says so.

03

Propose

You get a ticket you can read line by line : rate, fees, risk. Nothing is signed or sent until you hold the button yourself.

Questions people actually ask before they hold the button.

Straight answers about how Lyra works, what it can see, and what it can never touch. If something's missing, the real agent inside the app can look it up live.

Lyra is an iWallet (an intelligent wallet), non-custodial, live on Plasma, Ethereum, Base, and Polygon. Instead of clicking through a protocol's own interface, you describe what you want in plain language, and Lyra researches the rate, the fees, and the risk before preparing a transaction for you to approve.

Nothing about those protocols changes. Lyra sits in front of them. Instead of opening each site, checking a rate, approving a token, and setting parameters yourself, you say what you want once and Lyra assembles the same transaction as a single ticket you review and confirm.

No. You can ask in ordinary sentences | "swap 200 XPL for USDT0" or "what's the safest way to earn yield on USDT0 right now" | and Lyra translates that into a real, verified transaction. Every ticket still shows the underlying numbers, so you can learn as you go if you want to.

Yes. Lyra uses the same research loop as live web search and on-chain checks to answer questions like whether a contract looks risky, without preparing anything to sign.

Send from Plasma, Ethereum, Base, Polygon, or elsewhere, it all lands on the same address; if it arrives on a chain Lyra doesn't actively support yet, ask Lyra to consolidate it and it will route it back to your main balance.

No, never. Your key is encrypted and stored locally on your device. Lyra can propose transactions, but it has no path to your key, unlocked or not.

It's the one control used for every transaction in Lyra, on purpose. Reading the ticket and then deliberately holding a button for a moment is harder to do by accident than a single tap, so it's the same gesture whether you're sending, swapping, bridging, or staking.

Lyra locks itself automatically after 10 minutes of inactivity. It isn't a setting you have to remember to turn on, it's on by default for every account.

Four, today: Uniswap V3 for swaps, LI.FI for bridging (the same routing engine behind Jumper), Aave V3 for staking, and Polymarket for prediction markets. All four are verified, live contracts, not simulated, across Plasma, Ethereum, Base, and Polygon.

Yes. Every rate, fee, and yield figure is fetched at the moment you ask, never from a cache or from memory. If Lyra can't get a reliable live number, it tells you that instead of estimating.

Yes, staking through Aave on Lyra has no lock. You can withdraw at any time, using the same one-hold confirmation as every other action.

Predictions let you bet on the outcome of a real-world event, an election, a sports match, whether a price crosses some level, through Polymarket, the largest prediction-market exchange. Ask Lyra in plain language, e.g. "bet $20 the Fed cuts rates in December," and it finds the exact market, reads the live price, and prepares the order for you to confirm.

Polygon, Polymarket's home network. If you place a bet while active on a different network, Lyra still routes the order correctly, your position lives on Polygon regardless of which chain you're viewing in the app.

Yes. While a market is still live, open your position from the Predictions tab and cash out, Lyra sells your shares back into Polymarket's order book at the current price as a market order, same one-hold confirmation as everything else. An unfilled limit order can be cancelled the same way.

Nothing is stuck, claiming a resolved market's winnings just isn't wired into Lyra yet, only cashing out a still-live position is. Once a market you bet on has resolved, go to polymarket.com, connect the same wallet, and redeem the position there, that's the same on-chain wallet Lyra uses, so your winnings land right back in your Polygon balance and Lyra picks them up from there. This only applies to positions Polymarket has actually settled; if a market is still open, use Cash Out inside Lyra instead.

Yes, fetched from Polymarket's live order book right when you ask, not cached. Every bet executes as a market order against that same book, so the price you confirm can move slightly between the quote and the fill, exactly like it would placing the order directly on polymarket.com.

Privacy Policy

Last updated: September 2026

1. What we don't collect

Lyra is non-custodial: your private key and wallet data are encrypted and stored locally on your device. We never have access to your key, seed phrase, or funds.

2. What we may collect

  • Information you provide directly, such as through our contact form (name, email, message).
  • Basic technical data (app version, general error logs) used only to diagnose issues.
  • The text of what you type to the assistant, and wallet context needed to act on it, your public wallet address and displayed balance. Never your private key or recovery phrase, which stay local to your device and are never transmitted anywhere.
  • Live queries made on your behalf to check rates, routes, or contract data, processed at the moment of your request and not used to build a profile of you.

3. How we use it

Any data we collect is used solely to operate, maintain, and improve Lyra, and to respond to inquiries sent through our contact form. We do not sell your personal data. Our backend does not maintain a database of your requests: each one is processed to generate a response and is not retained afterward beyond standard, short-lived server logs used for debugging.

4. AI providers

To understand your request and prepare a ticket, Lyra sends what you typed, and the minimal wallet context described above, to an AI provider. Depending on availability, this may be OpenRouter (routing to a Qwen model), Mistral AI, Google (Gemini), or Anthropic (Claude); only one provider handles a given request, chosen automatically as a fallback chain, never all at once. Each provider processes that request under its own privacy policy and may briefly retain it per their own data-handling terms; Lyra does not control this.

5. Other third parties

When you use Lyra to interact with a third-party protocol or data source (currently KyberSwap and Uniswap V3, LI.FI, Aave V3, Polymarket, PlasmaScan/Etherscan/Basescan/PolygonScan, and CoinGecko), that service may independently process data related to your request, such as your wallet address for an on-chain quote or transaction. Their own privacy practices apply to that interaction.
